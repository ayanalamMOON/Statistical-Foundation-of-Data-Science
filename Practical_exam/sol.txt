Below are intuitive, well-designed prompts you can give to an LLM to solve your assignment without copying the question, while still covering:

• Implementation with explanation
• The prompt you used
• Reason for the prompt

I’ll give you 3 different prompt styles so you can choose whichever feels most natural.

Prompt 1 (Direct & Statistical)

“Perform a two-tailed one-sample t-test at α = 0.05 for a dataset of eight weight measurements.
Assume a hypothesised population mean of 50.
Compute the sample mean, sample standard deviation, test statistic, degrees of freedom, p-value, and conclusion.
Show all calculations step-by-step, including formulas.
Then explain the result in plain language.”

Why this prompt works

It gives the model exactly the structure needed: a hypothesis test with full derivation.
It avoids copying the original question but still contains all necessary test instructions.

Prompt 2 (Implementation-Focused)

“I have eight observations representing product weights.
I want to test if the true average weight differs from a target value of 50.
Use a two-sided one-sample t-test at a 5% significance level.
Show the implementation (Python or manual math), illustrate each formula used, compute the numerical result, and explain what the conclusion means in terms of quality checking.”

Why this prompt works

This version centers on implementation, which your assignment explicitly requires.
It signals the model to include formulas + explanation + interpretation.

Prompt 3 (Classroom Viva Style)

“Explain how to check whether a sample of eight weight measurements suggests that the population mean is different from 50.
Use a two-tailed t-test with α = 0.05.
Walk through the full hypothesis-testing procedure:
(1) stating H0 and H1,
(2) calculating the sample mean and standard deviation,
(3) deriving the t-statistic,
(4) finding the p-value,
(5) giving a decision and explanation.
Give the answer in a clear, exam-style manner.”

Why this prompt works

It mimics a viva explanation format, which ensures the output is written like an exam answer.

Prompt 4 (For Maximum Detail & Clarity)

“I have a small dataset (n=8) representing measured weights from a production batch.
The manufacturing standard claims the mean weight should be 50.
Help me determine whether the sample provides statistical evidence that the actual mean is different.
Use a two-tailed t-test at a significance level of 0.05.
Show every calculation in detail, including the formula for the t-statistic, manual computation of variance, standard deviation, and the p-value.
Finish with a clear explanation of the statistical conclusion.”

Why this prompt works

This version gives the LLM maximum control and ensures deep step-by-step derivation.

Prompt 5 (Minimal but Powerful)

“Here are eight sample weights: [write your weights].
Test whether the sample mean is statistically different from 50 using a two-sided t-test at α = 0.05.
Show full derivations and interpret the result.”

Why this prompt works

Short, elegant, and still complete.
